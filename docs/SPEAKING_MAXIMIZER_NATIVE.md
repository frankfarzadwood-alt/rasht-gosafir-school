# Speaking Maximizer: native speaking curriculum

This is the eighteenth and final book in the requested cumulative collection. It extends the existing TypeScript/React/TanStack/Vite app. The two new batches append after the earlier twenty-five BOOK_RELEASES; earlier payloads, school progress, mission boundaries, Evolve/Oxford recommendations and personal-work keys are retained.

## Coverage

- Three source speaking parts, nineteen interview themes, all one hundred numbered cue cards and all one hundred numbered discussion topics.
- Three additional native toolkits: interview/personal notes, long-turn organisation/linking, and critical thinking/self-review. These are explicitly toolkits, never fabricated source Card 101 or Topic 101.
- 1,320 objectively graded language activities: 888 contextual vocabulary placements, 225 sentence builders, one hundred discussion-function choices, nineteen interview-usage choices, thirty-two toolkit choices, forty reading questions and sixteen native visual questions.
- 222 unscored speaking rehearsals: twenty interview groups with one hundred independently written personal prompts; one hundred cue cards plus their toolkit; one hundred discussion topics plus their toolkit.
- Twenty independently written fictional response extracts for Cards 1-20, with two reading questions each. These are short analysis examples, not full timed speeches to memorise or reproductions of the supplied sample answers.
- Four native visual selections: an interview-answer process, original talk-planning table, fictional pros/cons table and fictional attendance chart for distinguishing evidence from a causal assumption.
- Three fixed twenty-question language reviews reuse canonical questions. They assess language knowledge, not actual speaking performance, official IELTS marks or band scores.

Coverage follows the book's topics and speaking functions. It does not claim a graded reproduction of every printed question, sample answer, appendix expression or teacher activity. Personal answers can express different views. Objective questions grade specified word meanings, grammar, stated information and explicit language functions; they never require agreement with a social or political opinion.

## Speaking studio and native language activities

Part 1 cycles through short interview questions and offers a forty-five-second practice timer per answer. Part 2 retains sixty-second preparation and a 120-second long-turn rehearsal, with a native cue card and follow-up discussion. Part 3 cycles through discussion questions with a ninety-second practice timer per answer. The brief-answer timers are explicitly practice choices, not official part-duration claims. Every timer pauses/resumes; switching tasks resets the stage and loads that task's notes. Checklists remain self-review, without an automatic band score.

Maximizer personal tasks activate only when their actual application/sentence-building questions are in the approved pool. A vocabulary-only filtered pool cannot expose an unrelated rehearsal. All three numbered parts have ordinary unit scopes, while their source topics use stable subunit IDs. Search in each part finds English or Persian topic labels. The two personality cards both retain searchable personality titles.

All meanings and examples are original or reviewed reuse within this new release. The 888 card count includes repeated targets in relevant contexts; it is not a unique-headword count. Animal welfare and historical-building conservation keep their appropriate senses. Habit describes an automatic repeated tendency, rather than duplicating the sequence sense of routine. Clear synonym overlaps are excluded from pair-only modes while remaining in vocabulary/spelling practice; each topic still has at least three curated pair targets. Word Forge retains the real letter shuffle and repeated-letter inventory.

The source contains dated forecasts, unsupported causal statements, stereotypes and prescriptive social/health claims. New practice does not convert those statements into facts or mandatory opinions. Topic discussions use neutral, independently written prompts. Medical, legal, family, disability, migration and poverty themes practise vocabulary and reasoning rather than personal guidance or group stereotypes. New diagrams and data are explicitly fictional. No new bitmap generation or editing is needed for this text-based speaking curriculum.
