# IELTS Vocabulary up to band 6.0: native curriculum

This release extends the existing TypeScript/React/TanStack/Vite app. It appends two batches after the earlier twenty-three content releases, in the fixed order Units 1-10 then Units 11-25. Earlier payloads, school progress, missions, Evolve/Oxford recommendations and personal-work keys are retained. The user requested one checkpoint per book and one cumulative Lovable source ZIP after all eighteen books.

## Coverage

- 1,972 graded activities across twenty-five retained unit IDs and one hundred native learning groups.
- 1,421 contextual vocabulary cards: 1,294 topic-wordlist placements plus 112 reference-unit targets and fifteen additional plant/animal-part targets from Unit 9. This is a card count, not a claim of 1,421 different headwords; source units sometimes repeat a word in a different context.
- 220 contextual usage, word-family, collocation, spelling and skills choices; fifty sentence builders.
- 188 listening questions across all forty-eight supplied exercise recordings.
- Sixteen independently written reading models with fifty questions, including everyday notices, a job advertisement, an enquiry letter, argument structure and evidence-based True/False/Not Given practice.
- Twenty-eight original chart/table/process questions across seven exact visual selections.
- Fifteen independent photo questions across five exact selections, using fifteen reviewed tiles from three previously generated atlases. No book-page screenshot or new bitmap asset is used.
- Twenty-five unscored writing tasks and six speaking rehearsals, in Units 1, 2, 6, 7, 12 and 20.
- Five fixed forty-question reviews for successive groups of five units, reusing canonical usage questions without inflating the question bank.

The twenty topic units cover relationships/families; development and memory; health/exercise vocabulary; lifestyles; study/research; communication; travel; history/time; flora/fauna/agriculture; space; buildings/engineering; IT/devices; globalisation/trends; city problems/solutions; environment/pollution; energy/resources; employment/business; law/crime vocabulary; media/fame; and artistic appreciation.

Units 21-25 have released practice for dictionary entries and word families; learning records/spelling/collocation; Academic Writing Task 1 data and process description; Academic Writing Task 2 linking/opinion/register; and General Training letters and essay language. Their practice is not replaced by an empty appendix or a generic topic unit.

Each unit has four native groups: words/meanings; usage/sentence patterns; reading/listening/exploration; and skills/application. The groups have unit-level source alignment and `source_section_claim: false`; they do not pretend to be four printed A-D sections. Every group is populated. Personal writing and speaking belong to released application nodes, so vocabulary-only, listening-only or unrelated filtered pools cannot show another unit's personal task.

This is coverage of the unit curriculum and wordlist targets. It is not a claim that every numbered printed exercise, appendix entry or personal response has an automatically graded counterpart. The five native reviews are independently authored curriculum practice, not transcriptions of the supplied tests, certified IELTS papers, official mark schemes or band-score estimates.

## Listening reviews and source boundaries

The extracted supplied scripts contain extensive OCR substitutions and damaged words. The raw extraction remains in the working audit files. It is not exposed in gameplay or silently presented as accurate dialogue.

Thirteen short pronunciation/word-family lists have reviewed supplied text: 3c, 5c, 7b, 9b, 10b, 11b, 13b, 14b, 17b, 19c, 20c, 21 and 22a. Their contexts have `spoken_transcript: true`. All thirteen lists were checked against rendered script pages; recording 13b uses sceptical, correcting the OCR's apparent identical.

The other thirty-five recordings use independently authored listening review notes checked against the supplied script. These are labelled as notes, explicitly state that they are not word-for-word transcripts, and have `spoken_transcript: false`. They are paraphrases, not invented dialogue, replacement recordings or missing-source claims. Review notes and supplied lists remain hidden until all dependent questions in the session have been answered. The shared audio UI names a review and a transcript differently, also respecting earlier Project releases which already contain honest answer reviews.

The 1c review retains the corrected childcare arrangement (Monday and Tuesday, grandmother on Wednesday, collection at four), rather than confusing the parent's three o'clock work finish with collection. 12a uses the fictional device descriptions in the source dialogue, not current product recommendations. 15b distinguishes the same spelling in different grammatical roles. 22b retains the council's ten undergraduates and four postgraduate students.

Health/development passages, historical remedies, simplified planet descriptions, energy estimates, dated market comparisons, forecasts and crime claims remain source-recording comprehension. The app does not turn them into personal medical/legal advice, current statistics or a modern scientific reference. New readings and visual data are fictional practice examples. Questions avoid endorsing claims about treatments, imposing a fixed developmental timetable or presenting historical remedies as instructions.

## Meanings and independent media

All 1,421 cards use independently written meanings. Of these, 640 reuse earlier independently authored definitions after review in the new context, with the old definition ID recorded for provenance. New canonical questions and vocabulary IDs remain distinct; earlier entries are not modified. Optional Persian glosses are retained where available, while the cards teach English meanings.

Context corrections include close and relationship breakdown in Unit 1; memory/mind/stage in Unit 2; fat/stress/stroke in Unit 3; mountain peak and transport in Unit 7; a historical timeline in Unit 8; plant cultivation and animal parts in Unit 9; meteoric in its space-related sense in Unit 10; operation/build/support in Unit 11; digital memory/platform/monitor/track in Unit 12; current and compound in Unit 13; waste/refuse in Unit 15; engine exhaust in Unit 16; employment candidate/company/casual in Unit 17; convict/guilty in Unit 18; and atmosphere/taste/novel in Unit 20. Artistic and hospitality retain their actual adjective/noun meanings despite source wordlist category errors.

Multiple-choice distractors are drawn from reviewed inventories with overlapping meaning clusters excluded. Pair-only modes use smaller explicit inventories, while the full card and applicable spelling pools remain available. Word Forge uses the existing real shuffle with repeated letters preserved and forward/reverse arrangements excluded when another order exists. Sentence-building prompts state their beginning and ending to make the intended arrangement clear.

Photo choices use the reviewed Evolve 1 actions/jobs atlases and Oxford Intermediate animal atlas. Travel scenes distinguish coast/forest/arid land; animal tasks show feathers, beaks, scales, fur and wings; device tasks distinguish laptop/phone/camera; arts tasks distinguish painting, guitar and singing. New metadata aliases use safe inset crops within a source tile; bitmap bytes and earlier metadata are unchanged. Picture positions shuffle while stable option IDs preserve grading, all required images gate answering, and feedback uses image names.

Original native visuals are a project timeline, club-registration chart, paper-reuse process, mini dictionary table, workshop-attendance chart, guide-making process and letter-purpose table. Charts retain ordinary responsive text and optional accessible value tables rather than shrinking labels inside a scalable graphic.

Writing keys: `english-quest-writing-ielts-vocabulary-60-unit-N`. Speaking keys include this book ID and the task ID. Personal work is explicitly saved and self-reviewed without objective XP/mastery or automatic band scores. Both earlier IELTS books and previous Evolve/Project drafts keep their existing keys.
