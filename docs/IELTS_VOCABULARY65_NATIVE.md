# IELTS Vocabulary 6.5 and above: native curriculum

This release uses the existing TypeScript/React/TanStack/Vite app. It appends one content batch after the earlier twenty-two releases. Earlier canonical payloads, school progress rules, Evolve/Oxford recommendations, package settings and personal-work keys remain unchanged. The one cumulative Lovable source ZIP is due after all eighteen books, following the user's delivery preference.

## Coverage

- 1,128 graded activities in twenty-five units and one hundred native learning groups.
- 686 contextual vocabulary cards, with independently authored English meanings and Persian glosses.
- 172 contextual usage, collocation, word-family and skills choices; fifty sentence builders.
- 169 listening questions across forty-three supplied exercise recordings and scripts.
- Eleven independent reading models with thirty-five questions, including evidence-based True/False/Not Given practice.
- Sixteen native chart/table questions in four exact selections. The data and situations are fictional practice examples.
- Twenty-five independent, unscored writing tasks and six speaking rehearsals (Units 1, 5, 7, 19, 20 and 25).
- Five fixed thirty-question reviews, covering successive groups of five units. They reuse existing canonical questions and do not add duplicates to the bank.

Units 1-20 cover character/psychology; time/change; individuals/community; chemistry/medicine vocabulary; study/work; advertising; travel; government/society; animals/conservation; space/physics; technology/materials; fashion/consumption; city/country living; problems/solutions; natural phenomena/agriculture; energy/resources; management/finance; crime/punishment vocabulary; media/fame/bias; and arts/taste.

Units 21-25 have their own released practice for dictionaries and learning records; reading evidence and question types; writing data/comparisons/cohesion; listening details/paraphrase/academic contexts; and developing linked spoken answers. They are not empty appendices or extra invented topic units.

The existing twenty-five unit IDs are retained. Four groups per unit organise native learning, rather than claiming four printed source sections: words and meanings; usage and sentence patterns; reading/listening/exploration; and skills/application. Unit 24 instead groups its actual numbers/details, meaning/paraphrase, academic conversation and lecture practice. Sections carry unit-level source pages with `source_section_claim: false`. Every group has a nonempty native pool. Writing and speaking are available only from their actual released application nodes, not from a vocabulary-only or unrelated filtered pool.

This is curriculum coverage, not a claim that every numbered printed exercise, answer-key entry or personal response has been reproduced or automatically graded. In particular the five native reviews are independent practice aligned with the five source review groups; they are not transcriptions of the source tests, official IELTS tests, official mark schemes or band-score estimates.

## Meaning and presentation review

- The source wordlist's apparent `draft` typo in Unit 15 is represented by the actual weather target `drought`, supported by the unit and track 29. It is not taught as a synonym for an early written version or a current of air.
- Unit 10's `astronomical` uses its large-cost sense. Unit 15's `current` uses its moving-water noun sense, despite its wordlist category. Unit 13's `migrate` and `settle` are verbs, and `petrochemicals` in Unit 4 is a plural noun.
- Related senses remain contextual: `cultivate` a relationship in Unit 6 versus growing crops in Unit 15; `state` as government authority; animal group nouns with their usual animals; `rotate` about an axis and orbital `revolve` around another object.
- Overlapping alternatives are excluded from vocabulary multiple-choice distractors. Closely related synonym clusters are also excluded from pair games, while retaining their cards and applicable spelling games. A definition should not make a plausible synonym a penalised distractor.
- Word Forge uses the shared real shuffle, preserving repeated letters and excluding the answer's forward/reverse order when another arrangement exists.
- Data charts use ordinary responsive HTML labels and optional accessible tables. No scaled SVG chart fonts, textbook screenshots or new bitmap assets are used.
- Writing keys are `english-quest-writing-ielts-vocabulary-65-unit-N`. Speaking keys include this book ID and the individual task ID. Both are explicitly saved personal work with self-review, no automatic band score or objective rewards.
